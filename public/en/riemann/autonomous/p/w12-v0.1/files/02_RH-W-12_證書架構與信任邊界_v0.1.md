## 1. Certificate Hierarchy

This package divides the computation into three layers.

### 1.1 Exploratory Layer

Uses NumPy/SciPy to compute generalized eigenvalues and lowest modes, and is solely responsible for:

- Selecting parameters;
- Displaying channel ratios;
- Generating rational witness candidates;
- Computing visualization attributions.

Floating-point results cannot change the GAP status.

### 1.2 Interval Construction Layer

Uses:

- `fractions.Fraction`;
- Rational $log$/$atan$ series;
- Integer square root enclosures;
- Outward-rounded `Decimal.exp`;
- Degree-specific Archimedean derivative tail bounds;
- Complete prime-power enumeration.

Outputs `mixed_10x10_interval.json`.

### 1.3 Exact Verifier

`verify_mixed_dictionary.py` does not place trust in the exploratory eigenvalues, but instead:

1. Reconstructs the exact Gram matrix;
2. Verifies that the Gram matrix is symmetric and positive definite;
3. Reconstructs the prime-power set $\{2,3,4\}$;
4. Reconstructs the activation maps for the three blocks;
5. Recomputes the quantization centers and interval row radii;
6. Performs a purely rational $LDL^T$ decomposition on
   $$
   C-\delta G-\epsilon I
   $$
7. Recomputes the isolated channel certificates;
8. Recomputes the interval quadratic forms of the rational witnesses;
9. Recomputes the self/cross decomposition and prime-power decomposition.

## 3. Interval Perturbation Principle

Let the quantization center of the interval matrix family be $C$, and let:

$$
\epsilon=
\max_i\sum_j
\max\{|C_{ij}-L_{ij}|,|U_{ij}-C_{ij}|\}.
$$

For any true matrix $M$ within the interval family, we have:

$$
\|M-C\|_2\le\epsilon.
$$

If the exact verifier proves:

$$
C-\delta G-\epsilon I\succ0,
$$

then:

$$
M-\delta G\succ0.
$$

Therefore:

$$
c^TMc>\delta c^TGc
$$

holds for all non-zero $c$.

## 4. Mixed Witness Upper Bound

The rational witness $c\in\mathbb Z^{10}$ does not rely on the floating-point verifier.

The verifier directly computes using integer multiplication and rational interval addition:

$$
[c^TMc]_{
m IV}
$$

and exactly computes:

$$
c^TGc\in\mathbb Q.
$$

If:

$$
\sup[c^TMc]_{
m IV}
<\frac1{1000}c^TGc,
$$

then by Rayleigh–Ritz:

$$
\lambda_{\min}(M,G)<\frac1{1000}.
$$

This upper bound does not require trusting the numerical eigenvectors; the numerical vectors are only used to find integer candidates.

## 7. Archimedean Tail Bound Contract

For degree $r=3,5,7$:

- Retain $F^{(0)},F^{(2)},\ldots,F^{(r-1)}$;
- Perform rational continuation on the corresponding $a^{-(2j+1)}$ tail sums;
- Bound the $a^{-(r+1)}$ remainder using the piecewise sup of $F^{(r)}$;
- For the leading $F(0)$ tail, use
  $$
  \sum_{k\ge0}\left(\frac1{2k+1/2}-\frac1{2k+1}\right)
  =\frac\pi4+\frac{\log2}{2}.
  $$

The current program is not a Lean/Coq/Isabelle formal proof. The trust boundaries include:

- CPython `Fraction` integer arithmetic;
- `Decimal.exp` correct rounding contract and outward expansion;
- The Python implementation conforms to the mathematical formulas in the documentation;
- The operating system and hardware have not experienced undetected errors.
