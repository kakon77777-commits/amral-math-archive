## Package Integrity and Mathematical Validity Must Be Separated

The historical 5×5 JSON of W-06 preserved the positive pivots, row radii, and witness sign decompositions, but the original verifier also required a 2×2 JSON that was not included in the public directory. The backend did not speculate or recreate this file, but instead:

1. Verified the independently checkable arithmetic within the existing 5×5 summary;
2. Logged the missing filename;
3. Marked the entire round as `LEGACY_INCOMPLETE`;
4. Prohibited the aggregate report from counting it as fully `VERIFIED`.

This is a publicly disclosable failure data point.

## Why the Status of W-14 is Not a Failure

The original derivative derivation in W-14 indeed had a gap, but W-15 has supplemented it with

$$
\int_R^\infty\frac{dx}{e^x-e^{-x}}=\operatorname{artanh}(e^{-R})
$$

and re-executed the exact LDL with the corrected bound. Therefore, the correct status is neither "the original certificate is fully valid" nor "the entire conclusion is invalidated," but rather:

$$
\boxed{\texttt{SUPERSEDED\_RECERTIFIED}}.
$$

## Red-team

`redteam_backend.py` verifies three types of attacks:

- File tampering without recalculating the hash: rejected at the integrity layer;
- Semantic parameter mismatch with recalculated hash: rejected at the native verifier layer;
- Elevating the manifest to `RH_CLAIM=true`: rejected at the claim firewall.

Therefore, SHA-256 is only responsible for artifact identity and is not mistakenly treated as a proof of mathematical correctness.
