# Core Computation Validation Snapshot

**Date:** July 23, 2026  
**Environment:** Python, mpmath, 50 decimal digits of precision  
**Status:** Regression test; not evidence for RH

Executing `validate_core.py` yields the following results.

## 1. Jacobi Theta Transformation

For $t\in\{0.3,0.7,1.5,3.0\}$, check

$$
\theta(1/t)-\sqrt t\,\theta(t).
$$

The absolute error is between $0$ and approximately $2.7\times10^{-51}$.

## 2. Mellin–Theta Representation

Compare

$$
\int_1^\infty
\left[t^{s/2-1}+t^{(1-s)/2-1}\right]\psi(t)\,dt
-\frac1s-\frac1{1-s}
$$

with

$$
\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s).
$$

At $s=2,3,4.5$ and near the first non-trivial zero, the error is within the computational precision range of approximately $10^{-51}$ to $10^{-54}$.

## 3. Log-Gaussian Weights

Taking $\lambda=0.01$, $p=2$, $q=3$:

$$
\left|g_\lambda(6)-g_\lambda(2)g_\lambda(3)\right|
\approx
0.00378426306283606.
$$

The difference between the closed-form ratio and the direct computation is approximately $10^{-51}$.

## 4. Argument Principle

For the rectangle

$$
0\leq\operatorname{Re}(s)\leq1,
\qquad
1\leq\operatorname{Im}(s)\leq30,
$$

numerically computing

$$
\frac1{2\pi i}
\oint\frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)}\,ds
$$

yields

$$
2.9999999999999995+0i,
$$

which is consistent with the region containing three non-trivial zeros.

## Note

These results only confirm the formula implementations and partial computations from the old manuscript. Standard high-precision floating-point integration is not equivalent to an interval arithmetic certificate; to upgrade this to a rigorous computational result, complete error bounds and a proof of no zeros on the boundary are still required.
