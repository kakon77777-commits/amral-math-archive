# 1. Scanning Targets

For each support radius $a>0$, consider the nested finite-dimensional spaces:

$$
V_{1,a}\subset V_{2,a}\subset\cdots\subset C_c^\infty(-a,a).
$$

Candidate bases may include:

- Translations and modulations of compactly supported bump functions;
- Smoothed B-splines;
- Orthogonal polynomials on a fixed support multiplied by a flat cutoff;
- Wavelet or finite element bases.

The first engineering version uses flat bump atoms:

$$
\psi_{c,\sigma,\tau}(u)
=
\eta\!\left(\frac{u-c}{\sigma}\right)e^{i\tau u},
$$

where:

$$
\eta(q)=
\begin{cases}
\exp\!\left(-\dfrac1{1-q^2}\right),&|q|<1,\\
0,&|q|\ge1.
\end{cases}
$$

and $[c-\sigma,c+\sigma]\subset(-a,a)$.

# 2. Matrix Interfaces

Define the $L^2$ Gram matrix:

$$
G_{ij}
=
\int_{-a}^{a}
\psi_i(u)\overline{\psi_j(u)}\,du.
$$

The Weil Gram matrix can be implemented in two ways.

## 2.1 Zero-Side Implementation

$$
M^{(Z)}_{ij}
=
\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}^{\prime}
 m_\gamma
\mathcal F\psi_i(\gamma)
\overline{\mathcal F\psi_j(\overline\gamma)}.
$$

Uses:

- Synthetic counterexample testing;
- Regression of known finite-height zeros;
- Direct generation of a negative witness, assuming an off-axis zero certificate is obtained.

Limitations: Real computations can only obtain finitely many zeros; truncation errors must be rigorously estimated.

## 2.2 Arithmetic-Side Implementation

First, transform to:

$$
g_i(x)=x^{-1/2}\psi_i(\log x),
$$

Construct the correlation form:

$$
C_{ij}=g_i*g_j^*.
$$

Then apply the explicit formula:

$$
M^{(A)}_{ij}
=
\widehat{C_{ij}}(0)
+
\widehat{C_{ij}}(1)
-
E_{B0}(C_{ij}).
$$

For a fixed $a$, the correlation function has finite support, and the prime sum only involves:

$$
\log n\le 2a,
\qquad
n\le e^{2a}.
$$

Thus, the non-Archimedean part is a finite sum. The true numerical difficulties are concentrated in:

- Interval errors of the Archimedean integrals;
- Stable computation of the removable singularity at $x=1$;
- Rigorous enclosures of basis integrals and the Gram matrix;
- Interval certificates for generalized eigenvalues.

# 3. One-Sided Certificate Logic

Finite-dimensional minimum:

$$
\lambda_N(a)
=
\lambda_{\min}(M_N,G_N).
$$

Since $V_{N,a}\subset C_c^\infty(-a,a)$:

$$
\lambda_N(a)\ge\lambda(a).
$$

Therefore, a negative value constitutes a complete certificate:

$$
\lambda_N(a)<0
\Longrightarrow
\neg RH.
$$

However, a non-negative value only implies:

$$
Q_W(\psi)\ge0
\quad\forall\psi\in V_{N,a}.
$$

It does not imply:

$$
Q_W\ge0
\quad\text{on }C_c^\infty(-a,a),
$$

let alone implying RH.

Each candidate certificate must contain:

```json
{
  "support_radius": "a interval",
  "basis_version": "basis hash",
  "dimension": 0,
  "matrix_arithmetic": "interval or exact",
  "M_enclosure": "machine-readable interval matrix",
  "G_enclosure": "machine-readable interval matrix",
  "witness_vector": "rational or interval vector",
  "numerator_upper_bound": "strictly negative",
  "denominator_lower_bound": "strictly positive",
  "prime_cutoff": "exp(2a)",
  "archimedean_error_bound": "proved bound",
  "normalization_contract": "RH-W-02 v0.2",
  "status": "CANDIDATE | CERTIFIED | REJECTED"
}
```

The simplest verifiable method is not to directly prove that the entire matrix has a negative eigenvalue, but rather to output a rational vector $c$ and rigorously prove:

$$
\sup(c^*Mc)<0,
$$

$$
\inf(c^*Gc)>0.
$$

This is sufficient to constitute a negative Rayleigh quotient certificate.

# 5. Convergence GAP

To approximate $\lambda(a)$ using a finite-dimensional sequence, the following are required:

1. $V_{N,a}$ is dense under the form norm of the Weil quadratic form;
2. Uniform convergence of the computational errors for $M_N$ and $G_N$;
3. Nestedness or controllable approximation errors;
4. Applicability of the Rayleigh–Ritz / min–max principle to the corresponding closed semi-bounded form.

Recent literature indicates that the Weil quadratic form for a fixed $a$ corresponds to a lower-bounded self-adjoint operator, and $C_c^\infty(-a,a)$ is the associated form core; however, proving that the discrete atomic dictionary used by a specific AI constitutes a form core remains an engineering GAP.

This GAP identifier is: `RH-W-04-BASIS-FORM-CORE`.
