# RH-W-15: Archimedean Out-of-Support Tail Correction and RH-W-14 Retrospective Recertification

## 1. The Discovered Gap

When establishing global bounds for the first-order parametric derivatives of the Weil matrix elements, RH-W-14 restricted the main analysis of the Archimedean integral to the finite support radius $R$ of the spline.

However, the Archimedean integrand is

$$
\frac{e^{x/2}(f(x)+f(-x))-2f(0)}{e^x-e^{-x}}.
$$

When $x>R$, although

$$
f(x)+f(-x)=0,
$$

the constant subtraction term

$$
-2f(0)
$$

still exists and decays exponentially. Therefore, after parametric differentiation, there remains a non-zero tail outside the support.

The original derivation missed this tail; the numerical conclusions of the original certificate cannot be retained merely on the basis that "the tail is very small," but must be recalculated and the exact verifier rerun.

## 2. Exact Tail Integral

We have

$$
\int_R^\infty\frac{dx}{e^x-e^{-x}}
=
\operatorname{artanh}(e^{-R}).
$$

For the first-order central derivative, using

$$
|\partial_cf(0)|\le\frac1h,
$$

we obtain the out-of-support bound for the even sum:

$$
\boxed{
\frac2h\operatorname{artanh}(e^{-R}).
}
$$

For the second-order central derivative, using

$$
|\partial_c^2f(0)|\le\frac4{h^2},
$$

we obtain:

$$
\boxed{
\frac8{h^2}\operatorname{artanh}(e^{-R}).
}
$$

All $\exp$, $\log$, and $\operatorname{artanh}$ are computed using rational bounding intervals.

## 4. Why This Correction Must Be Preserved

This gap did not produce false negatives, nor did it overturn the near-zero positive spectral band, but it is part of the certificate's trust boundary:

> The support function being zero does not mean that the normalized integrand containing the $f(0)$ subtraction term is also zero.

This rule applies to any integral certificate with local cancellation, global compensation, or a renormalization constant.

If one only checks the function support without checking the complete integrand, it is easy to miss the out-of-support tail.

## 5. Independent Numerical Cross-Check

This round also performed direct integration using 60-digit mpmath on representative degree-$3/5/7$ elements at the four corner points. The cross-check explicitly added the

$$
2f(0)\operatorname{artanh}(e^{-R})
$$

tail compensation, and all results fell within the strict W-15 intervals.

Floating-point cross-checking is not a proof path; it is only used to detect implementation errors.
