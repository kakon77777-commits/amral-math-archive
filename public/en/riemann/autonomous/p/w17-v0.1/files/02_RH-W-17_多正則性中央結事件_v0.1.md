# RH-W-17 Appendix: Multi-Regularity Central Knot Event

## 1. Algebraic Form of the Event

Let the furthest lag center be

$$
c(d)=-4d.
$$

For the negative sample at $n=2$

$$
x_2=-\log2,
$$

define the normalized knot coordinate

$$
z(d)=\frac{x_2-c(d)}{h}
=\frac{4d-\log2}{h}.
$$

The event surface is

$$
z=0.
$$

The left, event, and right states are respectively

$$
z<0,
\qquad
0\in z(I),
\qquad
z>0.
$$

## 2. Simultaneous Switching of Three Correlation Kernels

The mixed-order dictionary contains:

$$
beta_3(z),\qquad beta_5(z),\qquad beta_7(z).
$$

Near $z=0$, they all cross the central knot but possess different higher-order regularities:

$$
beta_3\in C^2,
\qquad
beta_5\in C^4,
\qquad
beta_7\in C^6.
$$

Therefore, the same geometric event has different visible orders in different channels. This is consistent with the kernel sensitivity-regularity duality in RH-W-11, but the current round studies **internal knot switching**, not the prime-layer soft start outside the support.

## 3. Two Types of Boundaries Must Be Separated

### Support Boundary

The sample enters the non-zero region from the zero region:

$$
beta_r(z)=0
\longrightarrow
\beta_r(z)>0.
$$

This changes the activation graph.

### Internal Knot

The sample switches between two non-zero polynomial pieces:

$$
P_-(z)
\longrightarrow
P_+(z).
$$

The activation graph remains unchanged, but the analytic representation changes.

The event in RH-W-17 belongs to the second type.

## 4. Why Saving Only the Activation Graph is Insufficient

If the compiler only records the active prime powers, it would incorrectly determine in this round that the entire master interval is a single chamber, because the active set is always

$$
\{2,3,4\}.
$$

However, the polynomial branch of the matrix generator has already changed at $d=\log2/4$.

Therefore, a complete chamber identity requires at least:

$$
\boxed{
(\text{active prime powers},
\text{sample-to-knot piece map})
}.
$$

## 5. The Role of the Event Thin Layer

Because $\log2$ is a transcendental constant, the computer cannot write the event surface as a perfectly equal parameter endpoint using finite decimal rational numbers.

This round adopts:

$$
[0.17328679,0.17328680]
$$

as a strict rational thin layer, completely containing $\log2/4$.

This design avoids two types of errors:

1. Misclassifying the event to one side using floating-point approximation;
2. Leaving an uncovered parameter gap between the left and right chambers.

## 6. Generalized Form

When $h$ is fixed, any event surface has the form

$$
a d+b\sigma+k h=\pm\log p^m.
$$

In the $(d,\sigma)$ plane, it is a line; after adding $h$, it is a plane in $(h,d,\sigma)$.

Therefore, future chamber compilers can write the set of event surfaces as:

$$
\mathcal E
=
\bigcup_{a,b,k,p^m,\pm}
\left\{
(h,d,\sigma):
a d+b\sigma+k h=\pm\log p^m
\right\}.
$$

RH-W-17 completes the first strict one-dimensional slice of this general framework.
