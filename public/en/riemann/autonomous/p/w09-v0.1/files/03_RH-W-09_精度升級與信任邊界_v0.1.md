# RH-W-09: Precision Upgrade and Trust Boundaries
## Why the $10^{-9}$ Candidate Requires Redesigning Constants and Tail Bounds

**Version:** v0.1  
**Date:** 2026-07-23

---

# 1. Why the Old Certificate Cannot Be Directly Reused

The certificate scale of `RH-W-08` is

$$
10^{-5}.
$$

When the explorer pushes the candidate to around

$$
10^{-9}
$$

the original two sources of error directly overwhelm the spectral margin:

1. The coarse harmonic bound of the Euler constant $\gamma$;
2. The single-step integral bounds for Archimedean power tail sums such as $S_3(K)$.

Therefore, instead of simply increasing the floating-point precision and declaring success, this iteration rewrites these two certificate modules separately.

---

# 2. Binet/Digamma Interval for the Euler Constant

Using

$$
\psi(n)=H_{n-1}-\gamma
$$

and the finite asymptotic expansion of the digamma function:

$$
\psi(n)
=
\log n-\frac1{2n}
-
\sum_{k=1}^{m}
\frac{B_{2k}}{2k\,n^{2k}}
+R_m(n).
$$

The Binet-type remainder has the sign of the first omitted term, and its absolute value is strictly bounded by the first omitted term.

In this iteration, we choose:

$$
n=100,
\qquad m=4,
$$

which means expanding up to $B_8$ and controlling the remainder with $B_{10}$.

This yields an interval width for $\gamma$ of:

$$
\boxed{
7.5757575757\times10^{-23}
}.
$$

Compared to the width of approximately $10^{-9}$ from the old harmonic double bounds, the constant error is no longer the dominant term.

---

# 3. Two-Stage Continuation of Archimedean Power Tails

For

$$
a_k=2k+\frac12,
$$

we need to bound:

$$
S_p(K)
=
\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}a_k^{-p},
\qquad p=3,5,7,8.
$$

The old method directly used:

$$
\int_K^\infty f(x)\,dx
\le
\sum_{k=K}^\infty f(k)
\le
f(K)+\int_K^\infty f(x)\,dx.
$$

Its interval width is approximately the first term $a_K^{-p}$. For $p=3$, this can still be larger than the error tolerance permitted by the near-critical certificate when $K=500$.

This iteration changes it to:

$$
S_p(K)
=
\sum_{k=K}^{L-1}a_k^{-p}
+
S_p(L),
$$

where

$$
L=20000.
$$

The first segment is accumulated using rational numbers, periodically coarsened outward to a fixed decimal rational grid; only $S_p(L)$ uses the monotonic integral bound.

Thus, the final interval width drops to approximately:

$$
a_L^{-p},
$$

instead of $a_K^{-p}$.

This modification only adds inexpensive rational sums, eliminating the need to compute tens of thousands of polynomial-exponential integrals.

---

# 4. Decoupling Computational Cost from Certificate Precision

In this iteration, we still only directly compute:

- lag $0,1,2$: the first $500$ Archimedean integral terms;
- other lags: the first $180$ terms.

The subsequent $S_3,S_5,S_7,S_8$ are only extended to $L=20000$ via a shared rational tail cache.

The result is:

$$
\boxed{
\epsilon
\approx
2.01\times10^{-12}
}
$$

without having to brute-force the expensive integral terms of each lag up to tens of thousands of terms.

This is the most important engineering correction in this iteration:

> Verification resolution should be improved through mathematical decomposition, rather than simply running the same algorithm for a longer time.

# 5. Trust Boundaries

The proof path uses:

- `Fraction` rational arithmetic;
- Rational atanh/Machin-type $\pi$ intervals;
- Binet/digamma signed remainders;
- Rational power tail sums;
- Exact $LDL^T$;
- The correct rounding contract of CPython's `Decimal.exp()`, further expanded outward to adjacent representable values.

Therefore, the label for this iteration is:

```text
RIGOROUS_NUMERICAL_CERTIFICATE
UNDER_DOCUMENTED_SOFTWARE_CONTRACT
```

instead of:

```text
FORMALLY_VERIFIED_THEOREM
```

If the next step proceeds into Lean, Coq, or Isabelle, it will require formalizing at least:

1. B-spline piecewise polynomials;
2. Normalization of explicit formulas;
3. Transcendental interval primitives;
4. Binet remainders;
5. Interval-to-spectral perturbation;
6. Exact $LDL^T$ positive definiteness.
