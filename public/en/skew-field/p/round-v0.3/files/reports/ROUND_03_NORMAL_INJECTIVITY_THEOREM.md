# Half-Turn Positive Curvature Normal Injectivity Theorem

Let a plane curve be parameterized by the tangential angle \(\alpha\), with the radius of curvature given by

\[
R(\alpha)=\frac{1}{\kappa(\alpha)}.
\]

Suppose

\[
0<\kappa(\alpha),\qquad R(\alpha)\ge R_0>0,
\qquad \alpha_1-\alpha_0\le\pi.
\]

Define the normal map

\[
F(\alpha,t)=\gamma(\alpha)+tN(\alpha).
\]

Then \(F\) is injective within the open normal band \(|t|<R_0\).

If two normal lines intersect at \(\alpha<\beta\), let \(\Delta=\beta-\alpha\) and \(m=(\alpha+\beta)/2\), then

\[
t+u
=
\frac{1}{\sin(\Delta/2)}
\int_\alpha^\beta R(v)\cos(v-m)\,dv
\ge 2R_0.
\]

Therefore, it is impossible to have both \(|t|<R_0\) and \(|u|<R_0\) simultaneously.

This theorem proves the global injectivity of the direct bidirectional normal band; it does not automatically complete the global reach proof for circular end caps.
